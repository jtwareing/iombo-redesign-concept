Studying bird migration for over 90 years

The Isle of May hosts Scotland’s oldest bird observatory, founded in 1934

Run by a charitable trust and manned by visiting volunteer observers between March and November, recording the birds and wildlife of the island from the Low Light, a former navigational lighthouse.

What we do

Recording the movement of birds through the island

The island is well placed to receive “falls” of migrants in spring and autumn, and the observatory visitors record the movement of birds through the island, including ringing them to learn more about their migration.

The Daily Census

The core work of the Observatory. Visitors spend part of their day, ideally the morning, recording all birds seen within three zones on the island, maintaining the long term data on bird migration.

How the census works

Ringing and records

The Trust provides rings so that observers can ring birds and contribute to the national bird ringing scheme. Records are published in an annual report and shared with NatureScot.

See the latest records

Stay at the Low Light

Accommodation for up to 6 people, usually staying for a week. It can be booked, at a modest cost, by anyone who is willing to contribute to recording the birds and wildlife of the island.

How to visit

Island wildlife

One of the largest breeding seabird colonies in Scotland, with over 40,000 occupied Puffin burrows, plus Grey Seals, Minke Whales and migrant butterflies and moths.

Stay at the observatory in 2027

The observatory is open from end-March through to early November. Those staying keep up the daily bird census and migration logs and record other wildlife sightings.
